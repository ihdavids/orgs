* Soil
** No-dig beds
   :PROPERTIES:
   :ID:       3b1f6f0e-5c1a-4b0e-9a63-1d2f7c9e8a01
   :END:
   The beds are never dug. Each autumn a few centimetres of [[id:8d4c2a71-0e9b-4f3a-b6d5-2a7e9c1f4b02][compost]] go on
   top and the worms pull it down over the winter. Digging breaks up the
   fungal threads and brings weed seeds to the light, so the less the soil
   is turned the better it holds its structure and its water.

   A new bed starts on grass: wet cardboard first, then fifteen centimetres
   of compost straight on top. Plant into it the same day.

** Compost
   :PROPERTIES:
   :ID:       8d4c2a71-0e9b-4f3a-b6d5-2a7e9c1f4b02
   :END:
   Three bays: one filling, one heating, one ready. Greens are grass
   clippings, kitchen peelings and comfrey; browns are cardboard, straw and
   the autumn leaves. Roughly equal volumes of each, kept as damp as a wrung
   sponge. Turning the heap once a month speeds it up but it rots down
   either way, just slower.

   A heap that smells sour has too many greens: add browns and turn it. A
   heap that does nothing is too dry or too brown.

** Leaf mould
   Autumn leaves stacked on their own in a wire cage, wetted and left for two
   years. Fungi rather than bacteria break the leaves down, slowly and cold,
   into a dark crumbly mulch that holds water and smells of the woods. Oak
   and beech are best; plane leaves take longer to rot. Bagged leaves with a
   few holes in the bin bags work too.

   Leaf mould and compost do different jobs: compost feeds the soil, leaf
   mould conditions it. Use young leaf mould as a mulch on the beds and the
   older, finer stuff in seed compost.

** Mulching
   Bare soil loses water and grows weeds. A mulch of compost, leaf mould or
   straw keeps the ground covered, feeds the worms and the fungi underneath,
   and stops the rain from capping the surface. Mulch after rain, never on
   dry soil, and keep it off the stems of young plants.

** Green manure
   Field beans and crimson clover sown in September on the bed that comes
   free, cut down in April and left on the surface as a mulch. The roots hold
   the soil together over winter and the clover fixes nitrogen for the next
   crop.

* Water
** Rainwater butts
   :PROPERTIES:
   :ID:       c27e5b39-61f0-4d8a-9e4b-7f3a0d2c6e03
   :END:
   Two linked butts on the shed downpipe, 400 litres between them, with an
   overflow to the little pond. They fill in one good storm. A lid keeps the
   mosquitoes and the leaves out. See the [[file:drawings/rainwater.excalidraw][rainwater drawing]] for the
   pipework.

** Drip line
   A gravity drip line from the [[id:c27e5b39-61f0-4d8a-9e4b-7f3a0d2c6e03][water butts]] along the two tomato beds. It
   needs the butts raised on blocks to get enough head, about fifty
   centimetres, and a filter before the first dripper.

** Watering in a heatwave
   Water in the evening, deeply and less often, at the roots and not on the
   leaves. A mulched [[id:3b1f6f0e-5c1a-4b0e-9a63-1d2f7c9e8a01][no-dig bed]] needs about half the water a bare one does.
   Pots and seedlings first; established beans and squash can wait a day.

* Crops
** Tomatoes
   :PROPERTIES:
   :ID:       e5a9d1c4-2f7b-4e63-8a0d-9c4b1e7f2a04
   :END:
   Sungold, Gardener's Delight and San Marzano this year, grown as cordons up
   strings in the two sunny beds, fed from the [[id:8d4c2a71-0e9b-4f3a-b6d5-2a7e9c1f4b02][compost]] bays and watered
   from the [[id:c27e5b39-61f0-4d8a-9e4b-7f3a0d2c6e03][butts]].

   #+begin_tip Pinch out the side shoots
   Every week from June: the shoot in the angle between a side branch and
   the stem. Keep the flower trusses. A cordon wants one stem and nothing else.
   #+end_tip

   #+begin_warning Frost
   Not out before the middle of May. A single night at two degrees sets them
   back by a month; keep fleece by the door.
   #+end_warning

   #+begin_info Feeding
   Comfrey tea once a week from the first truss, a litre per plant, diluted
   one to ten.
   #+end_info

   #+begin_faq Why is the fruit splitting? :fold closed
   Uneven watering: a dry spell, then a downpour. Water deeply in the evening
   and mulch so the ground stays evenly moist.
   #+end_faq

   #+begin_danger Blight
   Brown patches after a warm wet week: cut away every affected stem at once
   and burn it, never on the compost heap.
   #+end_danger

** Garlic
   Planted on the shortest day, harvested on the longest. Into a [[id:3b1f6f0e-5c1a-4b0e-9a63-1d2f7c9e8a01][no-dig bed]]
   with a little [[id:8d4c2a71-0e9b-4f3a-b6d5-2a7e9c1f4b02][compost]], a hand's width apart, pointy end up.

** Squash
   Three plants of Crown Prince on the edge of the [[id:8d4c2a71-0e9b-4f3a-b6d5-2a7e9c1f4b02][compost]] bays, where
   they can spread over the fence. Cure the fruit in the sun for two weeks
   before storing them.

** Runner beans
   A double row of canes on the north side of the [[id:3b1f6f0e-5c1a-4b0e-9a63-1d2f7c9e8a01][no-dig beds]] so they shade
   nothing. Pick every other day or they stop flowering.

* Pests
** Slugs
   Night patrols with a torch in May, a beer trap by the lettuces, and no
   mulch right up against the seedlings. The frogs from the pond do the rest.

** Aphids
   Left alone for a week, the ladybirds and blue tits find them. A jet of
   water knocks them off the broad bean tips if they don't.
